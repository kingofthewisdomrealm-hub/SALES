# Visual plan — Do this. Don't do that.

Nine steps. Each step is a pair: the picture you want, and the picture that kills the sale. Thought clouds are the test — if the buyer is thinking the Don't line, you are in the wrong still.

Jeremy Miner–style NEPQ. Questions first. Pitch last. They say the close.

Illustrated Word book (simple version + full pairs + stills): `Visual_Plan_Sales_Process_Steps.docx` in the project folder. Frame files live under `visuals/`.

Characters in the stills: **Jordan** (helper, green shirt) and **Gene** (homeowner, blue shirt). Same house — porch, then kitchen.

The field talk track after storm 3/16 uses **Josias** at the door. Same process. Different name on the porch. See `docs/GRADUAL_VERSIONS.md`.

## How to use this

- Cover the title on a still. If you cannot tell Do from Don't, the picture failed.
- In the trainer: Do stills are the stage. Don't stills flash when the player jumps the rail.
- On a ride-along: after the door, point at one pair and say which side you were on.
- Do is sage. Don't is terracotta.

## Simple version

Think of selling like knocking on a neighbor's door about a wet spot on their ceiling. You are not there to shout “Buy my stuff.” You are there to find out if they have a problem, and if they want help.

Over their heads are thought bubbles. Those are what they are *thinking*, not what they say out loud. If Gene's bubble says “Ugh, another salesperson,” Jordan did something wrong.

| Step | Do | Don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 At the door | Stand back. Hands empty. Ask if it is okay to talk. | Shove a big book of prices in his face. |
| 1 Be a person | Be calm. Ask one kind question. | Big fake smile and “You're going to love this!” |
| 2 Find one true thing | Look where he points. A brown mark in the hall is enough. | Stand in the yard and yell “Your whole roof is junk!” |
| 3 Let him say it's a problem | Ask if the mark is just ugly, or if it bothers him. Wait. | Scare him with “That's rot! That's mold!” |
| 4 Ask what “fixed” looks like | “If this were handled the way you want, what would that look like?” | Dump three giant packages on the table. |
| 5 Ask what happens if nobody fixes it | “If it rains again, what happens to that wall?” Then be quiet. | Wave a sign that says ACT NOW. |
| 6 Ask if he wants help | “Do you want this taken care of, or do you want to wait?” | Slide a pen over and say the crew comes tomorrow. |
| 7 Show only what he asked for | One photo. The hall. The one wet piece. | Open a huge binder with five plans. |
| 8 Pick a day | “Thursday or Saturday?” | “Give me money right now or I can't help you.” |

**The one rule.** Questions help. Talking at people makes them shut the door. If Gene's thought bubble is mad or tired, go back to the Do picture before you say another word.

## Full version — say / never say

### 0 · Approach

Do: stand back and ask permission. Don't: pitch on the mat.

- Say: Harbor & Ridge. Storm work. I don't need the whole afternoon.
- Never say: We're the full-service restoration company — let me tell you what we can do.
- Field version (Josias): Hey — I'm Josias with the storm restoration team. We're doing free inspections for the storm on 3/16. What I'll do is a quick check for damage, take pictures, and show you what I saw when I'm done. Mind if I take a look?

### 1 · Connection

Do: get curious, stay still. Don't: perform enthusiasm.

- Say: Mind if I ask how the house handled that last band of weather — just so I'm not guessing?
- Never say: You're going to love what we can do for homeowners on this street.

### 2 · Situation

Do: collect one true fact. Don't: diagnose from the yard.

- Say: When you walk the hall in the morning — any marks on a ceiling, or is it still looking the way it did last year?
- Never say: From the yard I can already tell those shingles are done. You're looking at a full tear-off.

### 3 · Problem awareness

Do: let them name the difficulty. Don't: name the problem for them.

- Say: That spot you wipe — is that just ugly, or has it started turning into something you actually have to manage?
- Never say: That's moisture intrusion. If you ignore it you're going to have rot in the decking.

### 4 · Solution awareness

Do: ask what “handled” looks like. Don't: dump the packages.

- Say: If this were handled the way you'd actually want it handled — what would “handled” look like? Not the brand. The result.
- Never say: Let me run you through every package we offer, financing, and the lifetime workmanship story.

### 5 · Consequence

Do: let them say the cost of waiting. Don't: fake the urgency.

- Say: If nothing changes before the next wet month — what does that do to the bedroom wall you already don't love?
- Never say: If you wait you'll lose everything. Storm's coming. Act now.

### 6 · Commitment

Do: ask if they want it handled. Don't: assume the yes.

- Say: Knowing that — do you want this taken care of, or do you want to watch one more season and decide later?
- Never say: Great — I'll send the crew. Sign here and we're good to go.

### 7 · Presentation

Do: show only what they asked for. Don't: open the whole binder.

- Say: Then the only piece that matters is the south slope and the hall path.
- Never say: Wait till you see the lifetime story. Let me show you all five packages.

### 8 · Next step

Do: offer two times. Don't: ask for money too soon.

- Say: If we're doing that, is Thursday morning or Saturday better to start the dry-in?
- Field version after a claim path: If you want, we can call together right now. I'll put your info in our system and follow up with you on the claim.
- Never say: I can lock the crew if I can take a deposit today.
